Fixture and timing limitations

Authored navigation mode replaces cave combat with 20 independent short-wall patrol stations on a flat 384 × 480 world. Opposing waypoints force real detours; the actors are never teleported. Each actor must cross its wall outside the blocked interval and receive a measured completed route. The route-only workload has no missile releases, followers, melee or pursuit. It measures production planning, locomotion, separation and drawing, not cave rendering, target memory, streaming or crowd congestion through a single doorway. A route request alone is not accepted as evidence of successful navigation.

Mixed-workload motion: soldiers begin at supported positions at least 72 units from the player and 30 units from initial enemies. Every five simulated seconds they return to those same revalidated positions so following and engaging continue after warm-up. This synthetic maintenance clears pending windups and visual history, preserves cooldowns, and is reported separately. Teleport distance never counts as NPC movement.

This is a seeded cave combat workload, not a complete playthrough or a minimum-device certification. Before each real simulation tick the player is returned to a validated supported anchor, with velocity cleared, so crowd pressure cannot turn this steady-state test into a chunk-streaming test. Player collision and pushback still execute normally. Enemies and player receive health refills to keep combat alive; shots bypass mana/cooldown input at a fixed simulation-tick cadence. Mixed mode adds supported followers, refills soldier health, and requires their actual movement and attack activity. Slime invulnerability remains its production policy. AI, support, separation, projectile collision, impacts and rendering are production code. A tower is deliberately absent: a market marker changes enemy safe-zone behavior, and a separate tower fixture must validate its elevated firing clearance. Production projectile console logging remains enabled (up to 30 lines per simulation second); its cost is included. Gameplay uses the fixed simulation clock; native performance.now measures every reported timing. The player pin and scripted camera heading are applied before interpolation capture: this measures moving-entity overhead, not camera-translation smoothness. JavaScript/Canvas submission time is not GPU completion time. RAF cadence includes browser scheduling. Compare multiple runs with the same fixture version, workload, view, resolution, counters and machine; earlier fixture timings are not directly comparable with v5. Toggle only interpolation for an on/off comparison. Snapshot timing combines capture and finish for the same tick; adapter timing subtracts that frame's inner drawing time from its wrapper duration and includes timer-call overhead. Do not add stage percentiles together. Disable coarse timers for a paired instrumentation-overhead baseline; outer frame, update, rendering and interpolation measurements remain active.
